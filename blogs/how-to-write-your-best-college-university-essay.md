If you are applying to college or university this year then you have probably started worrying about the CommonApp essay if you’re applying to American universities and/ or the UCAS statement if you’re applying to UK universities. If you’re applying to both, as is increasingly the case, you’re probably wondering how much of them can intersect. And the good news is: quite a bit.

Assuming you have the grades, you are probably targeting colleges and universities that populate the top rungs of the rankings ladder. You’ve probably also managed to confuse yourself with all the different social media accounts you’ve been following and that use keywords such as “spike” and “research” just to make you a little more nervous about the whole process and spend a bit longer on their page.

To start with, it would help to understand what these different university systems look for:

- Top US universities are looking to foster future leaders;

- Top UK universities are looking to foster future innovators/ researchers.

So, before you sit down to write, ask yourself this question:

For the US: how have I used my intelligence and my curiosity to contribute to my environment? More than where you are now, top US universities want to understand your potential as a leader, so even if you worry that you have not done much by way of activities by the time you apply, imagine this: given access to some of the best resources available, what could you achieve in four years? American universities see themselves as beacons to the rest of the world, what do you envisage your contribution to societies could be once you graduate? Your essay then seeks to answer this question: how do you know this?

The answer then could be tailored along the line of: I know that given access to some of the best resources in the world I could do (this) because with the resources I have had so far, I have done (this) and (this).

Your essay becomes about your learnings about yourself and the world through these actions/ activities/ events.

For the UK: the UK essay tends to be a lot simpler in terms of structure but much more academic, which some students find difficult. The question to think of is: how have I used my intelligence and my curiosity to further my knowledge? What am I curious about and what have I done to learn more about it? For top UK universities what matters is that you are a self-learner. You are able to formulate an independent thought and follow it home so to speak.

The answer to the three questions of the UCAS essay could then be:

1) I know I want to study course (x) because I am curious about question (y). So far, I have done my own digging and my understanding so far has led me to (z). Course (x) then becomes instrumental to you reaching your answer even if, most probably, your question will change within three years.

2) I know that I will enjoy this course because through my school courses I have particularly enjoyed (these) specific modules. These modules also help prepare me for the course because their intersection with (these) modules and the combination of (these) subjects, allow interdisciplinarity that further enhances my learning.

3) Because I am curious and a good student, I have not allowed school to limit my education and I have done (this) and (this) on the side that will also help me with my chosen course because they have taught me (this) and (this). And the combination of points 1, 2 and 3 will make sure I come out of your university as a top student!

In short, for the US: this is who I am as a person and this is my potential as a future leader. For the UK: this is who I am as a student and this is my potential as a future thinker/ innovator in my chosen field.

Where they intersect:

Both essays require you to have a basic understanding of the world you live in — you choose how big or small that world is. It could be your family, your neighbourhood, your school, your country or the world. Or the universe. Or beyond. You could choose to gain this understanding through a political, economic, scientific, mathematical, physical, musical, artistic, literary, historical, philosophical, or human perspective, or other. In the case of the UK, that perspective needs to directly related to your chosen course of study. In the US, it is helpful if your perspective and your preferred major converge, as it shows a certain clarity of thought, but it is not necessary.

Both essays require that you are a good thinker, not a prose writer. Many people confuse ornate writing with good writing. A good essay is a clear essay that conveys this message: “this is how I’ve grown/ this is what I’ve learnt” versus “this is how beautifully I write”.
