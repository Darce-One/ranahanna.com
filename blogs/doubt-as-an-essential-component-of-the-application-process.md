I have an uncanny eye for spotting when students I work with have used AI in writing their essays and applications, and it is this: the total absence of doubt. They sound too sure of themselves. Somehow, they seem perfect, their past experiences flawless, their knowledge on point, their insights refined, their trajectories seamless. Their essays read not as a point of reflection but as a roster of all that is good in them and their choices so far. And they are tone-deaf (the essays, not the students).

Ironic, really. Perfection may impress, but it doesn’t breathe. Leonard Cohen, singer, songwriter, poet and novelist said it best: “There is a crack, a crack in everything, that’s how the lights get in.” Doubt is what signals curiosity, humility and room to grow.

In seeking to impress, applicants are leaving no room for questioning. Instead of coming across as the perfect candidate, they are projecting the image of the perfect person, one who no longer needs to be educated.

Doubt, like much of what matters in life, is nuanced. Definitions include calling the truth into question, lack confidence in, or consider unlikely certain claims or events. Doubt can also mean uncertainty of belief or opinion that often interferes with decision-making, a deliberate suspension of judgement, a state of affairs giving rise to uncertainty, hesitation or suspense.

From a biological point of view, doubt keeps us safe: a creature entirely without doubt may walk straight into the jaws of a predator. Psychologically, it is characterised by the discomfort of holding conflicting ideas. Philosophically, to doubt is to admit that perception and reason have limits and that truth must be continually tested. Whatever the discipline, a common idea prevails: doubt creates the small pause from which genuine thought emerges.

Neuroscientists may have said it best: doubt is essentially an error signal, a flicker of uncertainty that forces re-evaluation before committing to an action or belief. From a neuroscientific point of view, doubt keeps us humble. Doubt is not a flaw in human cognition, it is the feature that makes knowledge possible.

In a personal statement or essay this translates simply as: this is what I know so far, this is the knowledge that I have acquired, and it has raised these questions, this is what it has made me curious about, this is what I still want to learn.

As with many good things in life, doubt is best consumed in moderation. What is required is a healthy balance between confidence and scepticism. Too little doubt and we risk danger, too much and we risk being suspended from action. All-encompassing doubt can be debilitating, but it is also what makes us human.

As ChatGPT said of itself: for AI to become truly reliable, it first has to learn to doubt itself. And is that not the essence of education? In an age where the value, purpose and cost of education are under constant scrutiny, it is perhaps worth remembering that education is not about certainty or the removal of doubt. Quite the contrary, it is about doubt itself, and how it leads us to our better, higher selves.
